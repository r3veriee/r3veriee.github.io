Year 3 Specialisation Project

Chromatic Decay

A first-person momentum platformer where velocity preserves the world's colour.

►
UnityC#Shader Graph

Role: Solo Developer — Design, Programming, Art & Audio

You play the Prime Packet, the last surviving data packet in a digital Network collapsing into a colourless void. There is no health bar: standing still triggers the Glitch, a shader-driven visual and audio distortion that stands in for a meter, so staying in motion is the only defence. Chasing that idea shaped a fully diegetic, no-HUD feedback system, an advanced Celeste-inspired movement kit (Dash, Super Jump, Wavedash, Hyperdash, Wallrun), and custom physics overrides to keep every tech-jump feeling weighty and precise.

Solo-built over a 17-week production cycle covering design, programming, art, and audio, and refined through an 11-tester Beta playtest that shaped a full post-Beta accessibility patch (brightness, effect intensity, and audio sliders).

Key Technical Systems

Motion-Linked Perception

Problem: make standing still feel dangerous without a conventional health bar or meter.

Solution: a screen-space static/chromatic-artifacting shader and a real-time audio low-pass filter, both driven continuously off the player's speed rather than a depleting resource.

Frame-Perfect Input Buffering

Problem: Wavedash and Super Jump require a dash-into-floor and jump on the same physics frame, causing dropped inputs.

Solution: a 0.2s Input Buffer System holds the jump command in memory and auto-fires it the instant ground contact is detected.

Momentum-Preserving Physics

Problem: Unity's default collision response kills momentum instantly on landing or hitting a wall.

Solution: custom, dynamically-reduced landing friction (down to 2%) for long slides, plus vector-projected wall contact that preserves 100% of entry speed into a wallrun.

The Trident Hub

Problem: confirm all three trials are cleared, in any order the player chooses, before unlocking the Tower.

Solution: each trial sets an independent completion flag; the Tower's entrance checks all three and only unlocks once every flag is true.

What This Project Taught Me

  • Diegetic feedback can communicate meaning without menus or bars — but it takes far more iteration to get the intensity right than a numeric UI would.
  • Engine defaults often have to be overridden: Unity's default collision physics actively fought the parkour feel I wanted, so ground friction and wall-contact behaviour were rebuilt from scratch.
  • Not every player has the same skill level — the Agility Trial's Boosthoop felt fine in solo testing, but 11 outside playtesters revealed how unintuitive its momentum redirection was without an early tutorial pass.
